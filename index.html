FPL Assistant ranks every Premier League player to help you pick the best squad for Fantasy Premier League. It uses expected goals, clean sheet probability, fixture difficulty, and other signals to score each player within their position.

The Score column

The Score column is the main output. Higher is better.

The score is a number between 0 and 1. It is a percentile rank — it shows where a player sits compared to everyone else in the same position. A score of 0.9 means the player is in the top 10% for their position. A score of 0.5 means they are average.

Important: scores are not comparable across positions. A midfielder with 0.85 and a defender with 0.85 are both good for their position — but you cannot say one is better than the other.

Adjustable weights

The six sliders control what the Score column cares about. Each slider shows its share of the total as a percentage. All percentages always add up to 100%.

Quick guide: For a reliable minutes-getter, raise Minutes. For a set-piece taker in a great run of fixtures, raise Set pieces and Fixtures. For a clean-sheet machine, raise Defence.

Advanced: Shrinkage K

You do not need to change this. The default of 400 is fine.

K controls how much the tool trusts players with few minutes. A higher K pulls their estimated rates closer to the positional average — making the tool more sceptical of small samples. A lower K trusts the small sample more.

Think of it as a "scepticism dial". Turn it up if you think early-season stats are misleading. Turn it down if you want to give a newly-arriving player the benefit of the doubt.

How to pick a transfer

A simple workflow:

  1. Select the position tab for the player you want to replace.
  2. Set Timeframe to Season to date and Horizon to 5 GWs.
  3. Look at the top 10 by Score.
  4. Cross-check xP/match and Pts/match — do they agree? If they disagree a lot, the player may be lucky or unlucky.
  5. If you are on a budget, sort by xP per £m instead.
  6. For the final pick, trust your judgement. The tool narrows the field — it does not decide for you.

Honest limitations

  • The model has not been proven to beat simple alternatives. In early tests it tied with "sort by last season's points per game."
  • Early in the season, rankings are noisy. The banner at the top warns you. Wait until around gameweek 6 before trusting the scores.
  • Scores are percentile ranks, not points forecasts. A high score means a player ranks well — not that they will definitely score.
  • Data is only as fresh as the last pipeline run. The timestamp at the top shows when it was last updated.
